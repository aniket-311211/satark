Why Satark

Name-only screening flags the wrong people

Sanctions, PEP and debarment lists are full of common Indian names. A screen that only compares names flags every Rajesh Kumar it meets, and each false match costs an analyst time and a customer a delay. Spelling makes it worse: राजीव संघवी, Rajeev Sanghvi and SANGHVI, Rajiv are one person to a reviewer and three different strings to a computer.

Satark screens every name twice: once for the name, and once for the evidence that says whether it's really the same person.

How a name is screened

Every customer runs through the same four steps, and every step explains itself.

  1. 1

    Read the name

    Transliterates Devanagari, strips honorifics like Shri and Dr, removes relation clauses (S/O, D/O) and legal suffixes, and understands initials.

  2. 2

    Match it

    Phonetic keys and weighted token alignment score the name 0–100 against 29,574 list entries, with a plain-English reason for the score.

  3. 3

    Check the identity

    Date of birth, nationality and a politician's term of office confirm the match, contradict it, or show there's nothing to compare.

  4. 4

    Decide, on record

    An analyst proposes a decision with a reason; a different reviewer approves it. Every step lands in a tamper-evident audit trail.
